## Operator errors, and what made them possible

| # | Error | What the procedure did | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | BOOT typed with the microSD still in | BOOTCHECK waited on the production address with no hint, then printed "microSD on another address? resume --sd-addr IP", which is the SDCHECK remedy and misled the operator | BOOTCHECK now recognises the microSD system on the LAN after 90 s and says the card was left in, with the command to power it off; SDCHECK finds the card by itself (this change) |
| 2 | Signing passphrase mistyped in R4TAG | the signature comes after a 6-minute precheck, and a failed signature reruns the whole phase | open: passphrases entered once at the start of the drill, decided with the operator |

## Finding: the microSD is a copy of the production identity

To tell where the Pi had booted, the host keys were compared: the microSD system presents the
production ED25519 host key. It was installed from production and, per restore.md, may also hold
the LUKS keyfile. Recorded in docs/known-limits.md. The new discovery uses it on purpose: production
is off from T0, so the only host offering the production key on another address is the card.

## Decision

1.0.0 needs a drill with no error at all (docs/versioning.md, tightened after C6). C6 does not
count. C7 runs after the passphrase change.
